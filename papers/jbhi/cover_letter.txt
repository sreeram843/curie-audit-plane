Innovation and significance relative to J-BHI scope
---------------------------------------------------
This work sits at the intersection of health information technology and
clinical artificial intelligence auditability. It studies a provenance plane
that reconstructs one bounded synthetic FHIR Release 4 to large language
model transaction—inputs, transformations, model configuration, evidence,
safeguards, and human ACCEPT/MODIFY/REJECT disposition—without storing hidden
chain-of-thought or raw protected health information in an immutable log.
The contribution is an engineering evaluation on synthetic fixtures:
independently verified reconstructability, tamper detection, role-based
access outcomes, ablations of provenance classes, and capture overhead
versus an identical unrecorded clinical workflow. The results are relevant to
biomedical and health informatics practice: they quantify what must be
recorded for a reconstructable clinical-AI handoff, and they keep FHIR
Provenance/AuditEvent as projections rather than a claimed Implementation
Guide profile.

Originality
-----------
This manuscript is entirely original. It has not been copyrighted,
published, submitted, or accepted for publication elsewhere. No preliminary
conference version of this work has been published. A related IEEE J-BHI
manuscript by the same author on retrieval-augmented FHIR translation is a
separate engineering study with a different research question and evaluation.

Human subjects / data
---------------------
No human subjects and no identifiable clinical records were used. The
reported evaluation uses synthetic FHIR fixtures and optional external
Synthea bundles that are not committed to the public repository.
