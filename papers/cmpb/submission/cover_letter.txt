Dear Editor-in-Chief and Editorial Office,

Please consider my manuscript entitled:

“Reconstructable, privacy-preserving provenance for FHIR-to-large language model clinical transactions: a protocol demonstration”

for publication as an Original Research Manuscript in Computer Methods and
Programs in Biomedicine.

Why this journal is appropriate
-------------------------------
CMPB publishes computing methods and application-software design for
biomedical informatics and medical practice. This manuscript describes a
bounded recording and verification method: typed audit events, a data/proof
split that keeps protected clinical payloads out of the immutable store,
structured model output without hidden chain-of-thought, FHIR
Provenance/AuditEvent projections, and an in-repository verifier. The
evaluation is a synthetic-fixture protocol demonstration of reconstructability,
tamper detection, access outcomes, and capture overhead. It is not a
diagnostic-accuracy or clinical-efficacy study, which matches CMPB’s
methods-and-software scope more closely than a clinical-outcomes journal.

What is already known
---------------------
FHIR Provenance and AuditEvent, W3C PROV-DM, and IHE ATNA supply
interoperability surfaces for agents, entities, and access trails.
Tamper-evident logging, hash chaining, Merkle inclusion proofs, and Ed25519
are established integrity primitives. Clinical large-language-model studies
have focused mainly on accuracy and summarization performance. Those
artifacts do not, by themselves, reconstruct one completed FHIR-to-LLM
handoff—inputs, transformations, model configuration, evidence, safeguards,
and human ACCEPT/MODIFY/REJECT disposition—without placing raw protected
health information or hidden reasoning in an immutable log.

What this study adds
--------------------
The paper integrates those known primitives into a reconstructable provenance
plane and reports frozen synthetic-fixture results: reload-and-verify
completeness 20/20 required fields; 19/19 labeled mutations non-verified
(18 TAMPERED, 1 INCOMPLETE); 11/11 access-control probes; mean paired
relative latency overhead 1.033 [0.788, 1.278] versus an identical
unrecorded clinical workflow including in-memory ACCEPT (n=30); allocated
storage 81,885 vs 11,373 bytes (relative 6.20; total 7.20×) and logical
storage 39,454 vs 11,373 bytes (relative 2.47; total 3.47×). Hash chaining
and Ed25519 are used as known primitives, not as a new cryptographic
construction. The measured path places one completed transaction root in
each Merkle envelope.

Originality
-----------
This manuscript is entirely original. It has not been copyrighted, published,
submitted, or accepted for publication elsewhere. No preliminary conference
version of this work has been published. A related manuscript by the same
author on retrieval-augmented FHIR translation is a separate engineering
study with a different research question and evaluation.

Human subjects / data
---------------------
No human subjects and no identifiable clinical records were used. The
reported evaluation uses synthetic FHIR fixtures and optional external
Synthea bundles that are not committed to the public repository.
