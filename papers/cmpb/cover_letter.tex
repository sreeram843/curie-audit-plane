\opening{Dear Editor-in-Chief and Editorial Office,}

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, ``Reconstructable, privacy-preserving provenance for FHIR-to-large language model clinical transactions: a protocol demonstration,'' as an Original
Research Manuscript in \emph{Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine}
under the subscription route (no author publication fee).

CMPB publishes computing methods and application-software design for
biomedical informatics. This work describes a bounded recording and
verification method for one synthetic FHIR-to-LLM transaction: a data/proof
split, typed events, structured rationale without hidden chain-of-thought,
and an in-repository verifier. What is already known includes FHIR
Provenance/AuditEvent projections and tamper-evident logging primitives;
clinical LLM studies have focused mainly on accuracy. What this study adds
is reconstructability, mutation detection, access outcomes, and capture
overhead versus an identical unrecorded clinical workflow on synthetic
fixtures. The contribution is an integration of known primitives, not a new
cryptographic construction.

This manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. A
related manuscript by the same author on retrieval-augmented FHIR
translation addresses a different research question.

No human subjects or identifiable clinical records were used.

\closing{Sincerely,}
